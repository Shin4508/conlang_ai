DECISIONS AND MATERIALS NEEDED FROM YOU

You can run the Python pronunciation designer now; these decisions are for the
next stage. Reply here or fill in this file when convenient.

1. Describe the first language you want to build.
   Who speaks it, in what setting, and what should it sound/feel like?
   Give 5-10 invented example words or describe sounds you like and dislike.
   Film/game references are useful for direction; choose your own sound rules.

2. Identify the performers and audience.
   What languages do the actors speak? Which sounds should be easy to pronounce?
   Do you need human recordings, synthesized previews, or just IPA and spelling?

3. Choose pronunciation conventions.
   Review profiles/example.json: allowed sounds, syllable shapes, word lengths,
   stress, and spellings. The current choices are illustrative defaults.
   A pronunciation specialist should review these before dialogue recording.

4. Supply an initial vocabulary or short script.
   Start with 20-50 meanings, names, or lines. Indicate whether grammar and
   translation are in scope or whether you only need naming/pronunciation tools.

5. Identify the sources of your data.
   Record where the JLPT CSVs, word lists, Polish TSVs, ONNX model, and generated
   word list came from, plus any licenses/permissions for your intended use.
   The downloader contains a FrequencyWords URL; provenance for the actual local
   copies and model training has not been established from the files alone.

6. Review flagged pronunciations before retraining.
   reports/data_audit.json identifies suspected source-script contamination.
   Arabic inputs and Japanese readings need language-aware review; do not just
   delete unfamiliar symbols from the model vocabulary. Correct source readings,
   regenerate clean IPA, and retrain/re-export matching model and vocabulary.

7. Choose the next interface.
   Is the priority a desktop tool, browser tool, game-engine export, or a Python
   workflow? The current working baseline is a Python CLI with CSV output.
